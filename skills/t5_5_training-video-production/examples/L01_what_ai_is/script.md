# Lesson 1 of 40 — What AI actually is

Format: 2:30–3:00, voice-driven, mid-college level, serious and plain. Each scene changes the picture on the words it explains; captions are burned in and also exported as .srt. Visuals are illustrative and labelled as such.

## Scene 1 — hook

1. You have almost certainly used artificial intelligence today.
2. A search suggestion. An autocorrected word. A recommended video.
3. But most people could not explain what it actually does.
4. In the next few minutes, you will be able to.

## Scene 2 — predict  (01 · THE PREDICTION ENGINE)

5. The chatbots and writing assistants you hear about are built on something called a large language model.
6. Its core job is easy to state. Given some text, predict the text that is most likely to come next.
7. Take the sentence: the capital of France is. The model assigns a probability to every possible next word.
8. Paris gets a very high one. Most other words get almost none.
9. It picks one, adds it to the text, and repeats. One word at a time, that is how a full answer gets written.
10. More precisely, it works in small pieces called tokens, which are often parts of words rather than whole words.

## Scene 3 — train  (02 · HOW IT LEARNED)

11. So how does it know which word is likely? It was trained on an enormous amount of written text.
12. In training, the model sees text with a word hidden, guesses it, and is corrected when it is wrong.
13. Repeated billions of times, this teaches it statistical patterns: grammar, common facts, styles of writing.
14. Notice what this is not. It is not a database that looks up a stored answer. It generates new text from learned patterns.

## Scene 4 — fluent  (03 · FLUENT IS NOT TRUE)

15. Here is the part most people get wrong.
16. A model built to produce likely text is also built to produce fluent text. And fluent is not the same as true.
17. Ask it for a source, and it can write a citation with a believable author, title, and journal that does not exist.
18. This is called a hallucination. The model is not lying. It has no intent. It is completing a pattern that looks right.
19. And its tone never changes. A wrong answer sounds exactly as confident as a right one.

## Scene 5 — goodbad  (04 · STRENGTHS AND LIMITS)

20. So where is it strong? Drafting, summarizing, rewriting, explaining, and organizing text that you give it.
21. Where is it weak on its own? Exact facts, recent events, arithmetic, and any detail where a small error matters.

## Scene 6 — rule  (05 · THE WORKING RULE)

22. That leads to one working rule. You stay the author.
23. Use it to get a first version, then check every name, number, date, and quote before it goes anywhere.
24. The more it matters if something is wrong, the more you verify.

## Scene 7 — recap  (RECAP)

25. To recap. A language model predicts likely text. It learned patterns, not a list of facts. And it sounds equally sure whether it is right or wrong.
26. Next lesson: how to ask, so the first draft you get back is actually useful.
27. Be the cog in the machine. Tools that work for you.
